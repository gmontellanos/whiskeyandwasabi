The Small Fix That Helps Google
(and Customers) Trust Your Business

Geraldine Montellanos · September 2026 · 5 min read

When your website, Google profile, Instagram, and LinkedIn all say the same thing, Google knows exactly who you are. So do your customers. That trust turns into calls.

Here's what it looks like when they don't match. Your website says you do HVAC repair in Sacramento. Your Instagram says "heating and cooling, Northern California." Your Google profile still has the cell number you used before you got a business line. LinkedIn says you're a technician at a company you left two years ago.

None of it is wrong, exactly. But nothing matches. And that small mess costs you more than you think.

Google is trying to figure out if you're real

Google doesn't just read your website. It looks around the internet for other places your business shows up and checks whether the details agree. Same name, same phone, same area.

When everything matches, Google feels more sure about you. That helps you show up when someone nearby searches for what you do. When details disagree, Google isn't sure which one is right, so it holds back.

Your customers are checking too

People do the same thing Google does, just faster. They find you in search, then tap over to your Instagram or LinkedIn to see if you're the real deal. If the services or the city don't match what they just read, they pause. A pause is often all it takes for them to call someone else.

When everything lines up, they don't pause. They call.

"Every profile should feel like a different door into the same house."

What needs to match

Your profiles don't need to be identical word for word. These five things just need to agree:

  • Business name. Spelled the same everywhere, including "&" or "and," and whether you use "LLC."
  • Phone number. One number, everywhere.
  • Location or service area. If you serve Sacramento and Elk Grove, say that on every platform. Not "NorCal" in one place and "Sacramento" in another.
  • Services, in the same words. If your website says "furnace repair," don't say "heating solutions" on Instagram. Use the words your customers actually search.
  • Website link. On every profile, pointing to your real site.

Platform by platform

Your website comes first

It's the anchor. Get it right here, then copy it everywhere else. Your name, phone, and service area belong in the footer of every page.

Google Business Profile

This matters most for local search. Your name, phone, hours, service area, and services should match your website exactly.

LinkedIn

Google shows LinkedIn profiles in search results, so your headline counts. Instead of just "Owner," try "Owner at Rivera Heating & Air | HVAC Repair in Sacramento." Add your city and your website link.

Instagram

Instagram bios don't do much for Google on their own. Their job is trust. Put your service and city in the name field, since that part is searchable inside Instagram. Then match your services and add your website link.

Facebook and Nextdoor

Same name, same phone, same area, same link. These are the ones people forget to update after a move or a new number.

The hidden piece that ties it all together

There's one more step, and it happens behind the scenes in your website's code. It's called schema. Think of it as a note written just for Google.

Part of that note can list every profile that belongs to your business. It says, in plain terms: "This Instagram, this LinkedIn, and this Facebook page are all me." Visitors never see it. Google reads it and stops guessing.

It's not a magic button. It's good housekeeping that helps everything else work a little better. If you work with a web designer, it's worth asking whether it's set up.

A quick checklist

✔ Is your business name spelled the same way everywhere?
✔ Is there only one phone number across all your profiles?
✔ Does every profile name the same city or service area?
✔ Do your services use the same words as your website?
✔ Does every profile link to your website?
✔ Is the old stuff gone? Old numbers, old titles, old addresses.

Your customers are checking. Google is checking. When everything matches, you look like exactly what you are: a real business, ready to help.
